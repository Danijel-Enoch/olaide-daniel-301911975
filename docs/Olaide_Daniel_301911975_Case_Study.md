## 1. Organisation

**Harmony Digital Microfinance Bank (HDMB)** — a *simulated* Nigerian microfinance
bank, used consistently across all five portfolio activities.

HDMB is not a real company. It is modelled on the publicly documented operating
pattern of Nigerian microfinance banks and mobile-money operators: a small branch
network, a digital-first customer base, high-volume low-value transfers, and a
small retail loan book. No real customer data is used anywhere in this portfolio;
every row is generated by a seeded script (see
`portfolio-1-database-design/Olaide_Daniel_301911975_Seed_Data.sql`).

## 2. Nature of the business / system

HDMB holds deposits for individuals and small traders and moves money between
them. Its core system is a **ledger**: a record of every naira that enters,
leaves, or moves inside the bank. Twelve branches in Lagos, Abuja, Ibadan,
Port Harcourt, Enugu and Kano serve roughly 20,000 customers, but most activity
arrives through self-service channels (USSD `*977#`, the mobile app, internet
banking and ATMs) rather than over a counter.

## 3. Users of the database

| User group | What they do with the data |
|---|---|
| Customers (via USSD / app / web / ATM) | Check balance, transfer funds, view statement, manage beneficiaries |
| Branch tellers | Post cash deposits and withdrawals against a branch till |
| Agents (POS) | Cash-in / cash-out on behalf of customers |
| Operations / reconciliation officers | Investigate failed transfers, reverse transactions, prove the ledger balances |
| Credit officers | Create loans, record repayments, watch arrears |
| Branch managers & head office | Branch performance, deposit mix, loan portfolio quality |
| Auditors & regulator (CBN returns) | Immutable audit trail, balance history, transaction provenance |

## 4. Major data to be stored

1. **Parties** — customers (with BVN, KYC tier, home branch), bank staff.
2. **Reference data** — branches, account products, transaction channels.
3. **Accounts** — account number, product, currency, status, current balance.
4. **Money movement** — transactions (`txn`) and the double-entry journal
   (`ledger_entry`), two rows per movement, debit and credit.
5. **Credit** — loans, repayments and their principal/interest split.
6. **Control data** — saved beneficiaries, append-only `audit_log` of balance
   and status changes.

## 5. Key operations the database must support

* Open a customer and an account; enforce KYC tier and one active account per product.
* **Transfer funds** between two accounts atomically — debit and credit succeed or
  fail together, and the account must never be overdrawn (Portfolio 3).
* Post a cash deposit or withdrawal against a branch till.
* **Reverse** a failed or disputed transaction without deleting history.
* Produce a customer statement and a balance as at a date (Portfolio 2).
* Answer operational questions under load: busiest channel, failed-transaction
  rate per channel, top accounts by outflow, dormant-account sweep (Portfolio 2).
* Serve a fast "recent activity" feed and session/limit counters to the mobile
  app, where a relational query per screen refresh is the wrong tool (Portfolio 4).
* Keep serving when one site is lost, and spread the ledger across sites as it
  grows (Portfolio 5).

## 6. Why this case study suits the portfolio

A bank ledger is the canonical workload for every topic the assessment covers.
It has strict integrity rules (money cannot be created or destroyed), so
Portfolio 1 has real constraints to enforce. It has high-volume append-only
history, so Portfolio 2 has real index decisions to make. Its central operation —
the transfer — is the textbook example of atomicity, isolation and the lost-update
anomaly, so Portfolio 3 can demonstrate concurrency control on a problem where
getting it wrong actually loses money. Its mobile channel needs document and
key-value access patterns that a normalised schema serves badly, which motivates
Portfolio 4 honestly rather than for the sake of the exercise. And it is a system
where availability and consistency genuinely conflict, which is the heart of
Portfolio 5.

The volume is deliberate. On a few dozen rows PostgreSQL always chooses a
sequential scan, and no genuine query-optimisation evidence is possible.
